# Better Docs editing check

Use this check after editing the complete draft. Mark each item pass or fail internally. Fix every failure before returning the document.

## Meaning and restraint

1. Does the edit preserve every claim, decision, requirement, condition, exception, qualification, and boundary?
2. Does it avoid adding product thinking, evidence, examples, metrics, rationale, or certainty that the author did not provide?
3. Does the edit avoid introducing, removing, strengthening, or weakening commitments and absolutes such as `must`, `should`, `may`, `never`, and `always`?
4. Are defined product terms, names, numbers, distinctive conceptual phrases, and boundary words used consistently and accurately?
5. Does the document retain its type, overall structure, authorial voice, and deliberate emphasis?
6. Is the amount of editing proportional to the actual writing problems?

## Communication

1. Can the intended reader understand the document's purpose without unnecessary setup?
2. Does each paragraph or list have a clear job?
3. Are important relationships, qualifications, and references easy to follow?
4. Are dense sentences untangled without removing useful nuance?
5. Are concrete facts and mechanisms preserved instead of smoothed into abstractions?
6. Are terms repeated consistently rather than replaced with decorative synonyms?

## Style and presentation

1. Are repetition, filler, empty transitions, vague claims, and puffery removed where they are not purposeful?
2. Does the edit avoid introducing binary reveals, `not just X` contrasts, negative lists, rhetorical setups, faux insights, colon reveals, superficial `-ing` analysis, synonym cycling, dramatic fragments, fake-profound endings, and recap conclusions?
3. Do sentence and paragraph shapes vary naturally without stacked fragments or robotic symmetry?
4. Are headings, bullets, and tables used only when they make the information easier to navigate or compare?
5. Does the ending stop on the final useful point rather than restating the document?
6. Would the edited document sound natural and precise if read aloud to a sharp colleague?

## Output

1. Is the source preserved unless the user explicitly requested an overwrite?
2. Does the response provide a tracked-changes or diff view that makes additions, deletions, and substantive rewrites reviewable against the source?
3. Does the response contain or link the complete clean edited document?
4. Is **What changed** short, accurate, and limited to meaningful categories of edits?
5. Are questions included only for ambiguities that prevented a safe edit?
